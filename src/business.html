Question 3–4

Would someone pay, and how would they find it?

The company pays

Someone who just lost their job is the wrong customer. The company making redundancies has reasons to spend (reputation, remaining staff, duty of care) and buys outplacement per person, from several hundred to several thousand dollars (Randstad UK, 2020; a provider's estimate). Yet 73% of redundant employees in one UK study got none (Personnel Today, 2010): that is the market.

Measured, not estimated (data). The real cost drivers are commercial hosting, the Adzuna licence for Italy (price on request; Adzuna, 2026) and any human support.

Pricing hypothesis

The app already sells per person in three packages: Essential (4 weeks), Standard (8) and Extended (12).

Price well below a coach-led package so a company can offer it to everyone leaving, not only managers; sell an hour with a human coach as an add-on. I still need to learn what HR compares it with.

How they would find it

  1. Outplacement and HR consultancies could resell it as the digital layer for people they cannot coach one-to-one: they own the buyer relationship.
  2. HR directors in a restructuring, reached on LinkedIn and at HR events, with the company view and its privacy rule as the pitch.
  3. Word of mouth from leavers who found a role; the site's invite-only waiting list measures interest first.

What could stop it

Job-data licences (Italy has too few offers without Adzuna), trust in the privacy promise, and duty of care: people in distress must reach a human, so the app stops and shows the Samaritans helpline (Samaritans, 2026).
