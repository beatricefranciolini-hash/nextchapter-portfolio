Question 3–4

Would someone pay, and how would they find it?

The company pays

Someone who just lost their job is the wrong customer. The company making redundancies has reasons to spend (reputation, remaining staff, duty of care) and buys outplacement per person, from several hundred to several thousand dollars (Randstad UK, 2020; a provider's estimate). Yet 73% of redundant employees in one UK study got none (Personnel Today, 2010): that is the market. The risks are job-data licences, trust in the privacy promise and duty of care: in distress, the app stops and shows the Samaritans helpline (Samaritans, 2026).

Pricing hypothesis

The app already sells per person in three packages: Essential (4 weeks), Standard (8) and Extended (12).

Testers suggested about €100 per departing employee, against an AI cost of about $1.83. I would price per employee, well below a traditional outplacement package, so a company can offer it to everyone leaving, not only managers. I still need to learn what HR compares it with.

Who I showed it to, and what they said

I tested the app with three people last week: myself and two other people, Federico and Antonio. Each had their own employee and company account, already filled with demo data, and went through Career Compass, the job matches, the tailored CV and the mock interview. They found it easy to understand and liked getting concrete career paths and real job offers instead of generic advice. Neither reported a problem; the real issues came from my own use (cases 4–5).

I asked the two testers what a company would pay. They suggested around €100 per departing employee. My measured AI cost for a full journey is about $1.83 per person, so the running cost is not the obstacle: the real challenge is reaching HR buyers and earning their trust on privacy. This is a very small, friendly sample, and friendly testers tend to be positive, so I treat €100 as a first signal to test with real HR managers, not as a validated price. My next step would be five short interviews with HR or outplacement professionals.

How they would find it

  1. Outplacement firms reselling it as their digital layer.
  2. HR directors in a restructuring, via LinkedIn and HR events.
  3. Word of mouth, measured first by the site's waiting list.
